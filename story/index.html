Why Holdfast exists

Independent companies should not have to sell themselves to gain the advantages of scale.

Holdfast is being built so owners can share corporate capabilities and market strength while keeping their company, identity, judgment, and control.

Shared strength without shared ownership.

The institution grows by making independent companies stronger—not by quietly accumulating control over them.

A pattern repeated across many kinds of business

Small and midsize companies are expected to need corporate infrastructure without having corporate scale.

Garrett and Rachel have worked across education, security, technology, creative work, retail, nonprofit leadership, and independent business ownership. The industries changed, but the structural problem repeated: good companies needed payroll, benefits, finance, technology, risk protection, administration, and outside expertise—yet each company had to assemble and negotiate those capabilities alone.

Large corporations solve that problem with departments, systems, specialists, and purchasing power. Independent owners are often offered only two paths: continue carrying the burden separately, or surrender some independence through consolidation, franchising, or sale.

Holdfast begins with a different question: what if independent companies could combine the strength behind their businesses without combining ownership of the businesses themselves?

The governing commitments

Scale should strengthen independence, not become an excuse to take it away.

These principles govern the business model, the provider network, and every department Holdfast builds.

I

Ownership remains the owner’s.

Holdfast grows by supporting companies, not by acquiring quiet control over them.

II

Collective value returns to members.

The leverage created by participation should improve member economics, access, service, and capability.

III

A department must be real.

Partner logos and referral links are not enough. Shared capabilities require scope, workflows, implementation, standards, escalation, and accountability.

IV

Recommendations serve the member.

A provider may compensate Holdfast, but payment cannot purchase Holdfast’s recommendation.

V

Professional work stays qualified.

Holdfast owns the shared system and coordination process; qualified specialists remain responsible for professional judgment and delivery.

VI

We build for durability.

The long-term measure is how many healthy, independently owned companies can become stronger and continue across generations.

The long view

Begin with a few useful programs. Build toward an institution independent business can rely on.

The launch will be narrow. The governing idea is not.

  1. 01
    Shared corporate departmentsPeople, benefits, technology, finance, risk, administration, purchasing, and future capabilities built for use across many companies.
  2. 02
    Collective market leverageMember demand organized into a purchasing and negotiating position that providers take seriously.
  3. 03
    A trusted operating standardClear provider expectations, transparent economics, useful implementation, and visible accountability.
  4. 04
    Portable opportunityOver time, benefits, training, credentials, and career pathways that can become more durable across the member network.
  5. 05
    Ownership continuityStronger options for family, management, employees, and independent buyers to carry good companies forward.
03How we begin

Prove the system before claiming the scale

The first members should help build the institution—not be asked to pretend it already exists.

Holdfast will begin with a small paid group of established owner-led companies, measure the common demand they represent, and launch only one or two department programs that can create real value and be delivered responsibly.

Founding members may help shape the workflows, provider standards, communication, implementation, and future department priorities. Every capability will be labeled clearly as available now, in founding preview, in development, or future.
